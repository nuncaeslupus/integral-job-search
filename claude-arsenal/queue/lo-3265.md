## What this is

Comparing a Spanish offer and a German one on gross salary is exactly the
comparison that misleads. The candidate needs a rough **monthly net** for each,
in their own currency (owner, 2026-08-18).

Two sources, in order:

1. **Rules committed to the repo**, per country and where it matters per region:
   bands, rates, social security, the obvious allowances. Offline, and no
   salary figure is ever sent to a third party.
2. **Rules generated when a country is absent** — the owner's refinement. The
   tool works them out, writes the file, and uses it. A search should never stop
   because we have not been to that country before.

## The gate is about honesty, not accuracy

A generated rule set is a model's best recollection of a tax code. It may be
wrong, out of date, or subtly incomplete, and a candidate turning down an offer
on a bad net figure is a real harm.

So every generated rule set carries `source: generated`, the date, and the model
that produced it, and **every figure derived from one is shown as approximate
and generated** — never presented like a committed, checked rule. The gate
counts the fraction of generated rule sets correctly marked; anything below 1.0
means a guess is somewhere being displayed as a fact.

Committed rules carry `source: verified` and a `checked_on` date, and go stale:
rates change every year, and a rule set nobody has looked at since 2024 should
say so.

## Not in scope

Personal circumstances that move the number a lot — dependants, joint
assessment, regional variation below the level modelled, pension arrangements.
State that the estimate ignores them rather than implying a precision the
calculation does not have.
