## What this is

The candidate-side facts the ranker must respect before any preference matters:

| attribute | shape | compares against |
|-----------|-------|------------------|
| languages | per language, a level (and whether it is a working language) | `english_demand` and its siblings |
| location | where they are, and the commute they will accept | `remote_arrangement`, the ad's location |
| relocation | willing / not / conditional, and where to | the ad's location, `travel_requirement` |
| salary | floor and target, gross annual, currency | the ad's stated band; `compensation_transparency` when unstated |
| availability | notice period, earliest start | the ad's urgency where stated |
| work authorisation | which countries they may work in | the ad's country |

## The rule that matters

A hard constraint the offer cannot satisfy **removes** the offer; it is never
traded off against a preference. And an attribute the candidate has not stated
is **unknown**, not satisfied — the spec's ranking risk (`unknown ≠ neutral`)
applied to constraints rather than scores. An unstated salary floor must not
silently admit every offer; it must be surfaced as a question the interview
(T27) still owes the candidate.

## Scope change — v2 plan, 2026-08-18

Two additions from specification v2, neither of which changes the gate:

- **T24 pins the `constraints.json` field set** and its three resolution states
  (`stated`, `declined`, `unknown`). The step specification names the states;
  nothing names the fields, so this task does. The *engine* that resolves them
  in conversation is **T41**, which depends on this.
- **Add the reach and legality fields step 7 needs**: employed or contracting,
  paid where, taxed where, and how far the search may travel (remote, commuting
  distance, relocation, working for an employer in another country while living
  here). Those answers change which offers are *legal* to take, so they belong
  to Sourcing's inputs rather than to a footnote at the application stage
  (`status/spec-v2-process.md` §2.6).
