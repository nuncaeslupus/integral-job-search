## What this is

Going back with new information revises what later steps produced rather than
throwing it away. What happens depends on which class the downstream artefact
belongs to.

| class | examples | on an upstream change |
|-------|----------|-----------------------|
| derived | `constraints.json`, `traits.json`, `weights.json`, extractions, annotations, rankings | recomputed from the evidence log; never edited in place |
| authored | generated CVs and letters, interview preparation notes | marked **stale** with the reason and the fields that changed; kept exactly as they are; regeneration offered, never automatic |
| historical | `evidence.jsonl` rows, `applications/`, `interviews/`, tombstones | immutable; never revised, only appended to |

The mechanism needs no new file: because the profile is a pure function of the
append-only log, a revision is `{"rows": <count>, "sha256": <hash of the file>}`,
and everything derived records the revision it was computed from. Staleness is
therefore **computed, not tracked**.

## The rule that matters

**The class that matters is authored.** A CV may already be with an employer.
Silently regenerating it leaves the candidate unable to answer a question about
their own application — so the old version survives, and the stale marker says
what changed and why it might matter.
