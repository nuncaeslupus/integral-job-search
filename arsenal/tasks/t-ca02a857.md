`connectors/ruled-out.yaml` grew a `policy_refused` section on 2026-08-31,
holding remoteok.com: robots.txt **allows** the endpoint under RFC 9309 group
semantics, and we refuse anyway, because the board restates those `Disallow`
lines verbatim for eleven named AI crawlers and the intent is legible.

The owner had already stated a different position during the session:

> About robots.txt, we already talked about that. It is the user who will use
> the scraper, not you, and most of them did not allow massive scraping for AI,
> but they allow it for making some searches. You should be able to build a
> scraper for this use without any problem.

That is a real disagreement, not a misunderstanding, and it turns on *who is
fetching*: a tool the candidate runs to look at a handful of adverts is not an
AI crawler ingesting a board, even when one user agent covers both.

**The refusal was written by the implementing session on its own judgement and
was never put to the owner.** That is the defect regardless of which way it
resolves: a policy that silently removes boards from a candidate's reach needs
an owner's decision recorded next to it, and `ruled-out.yaml` currently reads as
though this one has one.

Resolve it, one way, in writing. If the stance holds, `policy_refused` is
narrowed to what it actually justifies — probably *volume*, not *access* — and
remoteok is re-tested against it. If it does not, the section stays and says
whose call it was and when.
