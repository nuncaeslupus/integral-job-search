Imported from issue #545

## What

`matches_aim` is applied to whatever fields a board's list row happens to carry.
That is `title` on some boards and `title` + the **whole job description** on
others, so the same candidate phrase means two different things depending on
which board answered.

Measured today against a live run (16 boards, per-board ceiling, one candidate
with six aim phrases):

| board | list-row fields | items | added |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lever_en` | title only | 590 | **0** (1 on-aim, already stored) |
| `rippling_en` | title only | 613 | **0** |
| `workable_en` | title + text | 16 | 0 |
| `ashby_en` | title + text | — | **50** (ceiling) |
| `greenhouse_en` | title + text | — | **50** (ceiling) |

Re-running the same `matches_aim` over the **titles alone** of what those two
boards actually stored:

```
ashby        50 stored,  0 whose title is on-aim
greenhouse   50 stored,  7 whose title is on-aim
```

So 93 of the 100 offers admitted by the two description-carrying boards were
admitted on description text: `Account Executive - Enterprise (London)`,
`Offensive Security Engineer`, `Partner Manager`, `Lead Technical Instructor`.
Every one of them is at an AI employer whose boilerplate names the phrase
somewhere. Meanwhile 1,203 real vacancies at 16 other employers were reduced to
one match because their rows carry no description.

## Why it matters

Both faces are the same defect and both are expensive:

- **Title-only boards under-deliver.** `added=0` reads as a dead connector or an
  empty roster. It is neither — the endpoints answer 200 and the rosters are
  full. A session spends time diagnosing a board that is working.
- **Description-carrying boards over-deliver, and they hold the ceiling.** With a
  per-board ceiling (#541) each of them spends all 50 slots on rows matched by
  employer boilerplate, which then costs 50 detail fetches, 50 liveness checks
  and 50 rows of a ranking.

## Suggested shape

Match the **title** as the primary signal and treat description text as a weaker
secondary one, rather than unioning the two into one bag of words. A row whose
title is on-aim is on-aim; a row matched only in the description is at best a
maybe, and should not be able to consume a board's whole ceiling.

Whatever the shape, the gate should compare the same phrase set across a
title-only board and a description-carrying board and assert the admission rate
does not differ by an order of magnitude on rows drawn from the same employers.

## Evidence

Live run of 2026-09-22, 16 boards, per-board ceiling. Titles for the three
title-only boards fetched directly from their ATS APIs (1,219 titles).
