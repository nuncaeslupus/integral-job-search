## The defect

T159's sweep reads source. Five rounds of widening it — by name, by value shape,
by one delegation hop, by two, by bounded recursion to depth five, by committed
evidence file — closed real holes and left a residue the second reader
characterised precisely:

> the residual splits into 4 traceable, 5 not-floors, and **17 whose population
> exists only at runtime — no AST rule can ever reach them**.

A population that is assembled while the program runs is not visible to a parser,
and every further parsing rule is an enumeration with no last element. That is
the shape T159 kept meeting from the inside.

## The direction

**A floor declares the population it guards**, rather than the sweep inferring it
— discovered the way `repo_gate` already discovers evidence sources, by a module
declaring `EVIDENCE_SOURCES = (("T55", measure, record),)` beside the code that
counts. `repo_gate` finds those declarations by parsing and resolves them through
`sys.modules`, and names anything that declares one and is not reached.

The same shape here inverts the problem: the sweep stops guessing what a floor
bounds, and a floor that declares nothing is **reported by name** rather than
silently classified as out of scope. That is the only rule that reaches the
seventeen.

## What the metric counts

`floors_with_no_declared_population` is the number of committed floors that
neither declare the population they guard nor are named in a reviewed
out-of-scope register with a reason.

The register is the point: T159 shipped **55 floor-shaped names ruled out of
scope and never judged**, including `repo_gate.MINIMUM_FILES_FORMATTED` and
`MINIMUM_PYTHON_FILES_FORMATTED` — the two floors CLAUDE.md itself argues for at
length. Out of scope must mean *decided*, not *unreached*.

The floors swept are the denominator, a **literal** floor per T122.
