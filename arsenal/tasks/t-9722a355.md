These are the non-blocking findings from the second reader's round 2 on #878 (https://github.com/nuncaeslupus/integral-job-search/pull/878#issuecomment-6268042009). Under owner policy C/G they are batched here.

- **R2 (fail-open):** when the aggregator copy has no title, any posting by that employer is promoted. A missing title should not promote anything.
- **R3 (fail-open):** employer and title matching works by prefix or substring. A brief naming "Real" matches "Real Estate Staffing Agency", and "Backend" matches "Backend Sales Lead". Match on whole tokens or on normalised equality.
- **R4 (fail-open):** "This job has expired." and "Applications are closed." are still promoted, because `DEAD_PHRASES` does not cover them. Add them to the list.
- **R5:** running `resolve_canonical` again on an aggregator copy that was already promoted overwrites its canonical record with a fallback, and `primary_offer` then returns the archived aggregator copy. It must keep the existing promotion.
- **R6:** an aggregator copy with no lifecycle record stays `new`, so both copies are live. Affects only legacy or hand-made records.
- **Step 09:** the skill text should resolve to `primary_offer` explicitly.
- **Pages without JSON-LD:** these are recorded as `parse_failure`. Decide whether a text fallback is worth adding.

- **N1 (from #879 round 2, fail-closed):** `_UPPER_RAG` in `relevance.py` accepts only `[\s,/:&-]` between RAG and the status word, so "RAG – Status Analyst", "RAG (Status) Analyst", "RAG; Status Analyst", "RAG. Status Analyst" and "RAG_Status Analyst" are still read as the AI term. Use `[\W_]*` as the joiner and add those titles as tests.
