## Where this comes from

The candidate said her diploma was in Dietètica. I "corrected" her to Nutrició
and wrote two evidence rows saying so. She corrected me back, and unwinding it
produced a chain:

```text
ev-000048  Dietètica          (the candidate, correct)
ev-000060  Nutrició           retracts ev-000048   (wrong)
ev-000066  —                  retracts ev-000060
ev-000068  Dietètica          (restated, because nothing surfaced ev-000048)
```

`ev-000068` exists only because the store has no notion of an **effective**
value. Retraction is recorded as a row, never resolved, so a reader has to walk
the chain by hand to learn what is currently believed — and the way a session
avoids walking it is by restating the fact, which is how the same claim ends up
in the log three times with no marker saying which one is live.

This is not cosmetic. Step-11 requires every claim in a letter to trace to a
store entry. Tracing to a **retracted** entry is exactly as easy as tracing to a
live one.

## What to build

`EvidenceLog.effective()` — the rows that are currently believed, with retracted
rows and their retractions excluded, and a retraction-of-a-retraction restoring
the row it originally struck. Then have the step-11 traceability check read
`effective()` rather than the raw log.

Chains are short and the log is append-only, so this is a fold over the rows,
not an index.

## Tests

`test_a_retracted_row_is_not_effective`.

`test_retracting_a_retraction_restores_the_original_row` — the chain above,
verbatim, where the correct 2008 row comes back live without a restatement.

`test_a_claim_tracing_to_a_retracted_row_is_refused` — the step-11 check.
