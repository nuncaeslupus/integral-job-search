## F4 — the byte floor's slack pre-authorises an excerpting that has not happened

`MINIMUM_CAPTURE_BYTES_WALKED = 2_000_000` against a library of **153 captures /
4,058,112 bytes**. The floor still passes when the **7 largest captures** (2,058,112 B,
50.7% of the library) are skipped, or when the whole of `talent_es` is — and those large
captures are precisely where the wall-clock budget's cost lives, so a walk that dropped
them would be both fast and green.

The floor was lowered from 3,000,000 to 2,000,000 in round 8 to leave room for
`tools/excerpt_fixture.py` to excerpt both `talent_es` listing pages (1,159,687 bytes).
That reasoning is sound and the slack is real; what is missing is that the slack is
**unconditional**. A floor that leaves room for a future shrink permits that shrink today,
silently. The closed-rule direction is a floor derived from the library as committed
(a fraction of the measured total, regenerated with the evidence) rather than a literal
chosen to survive an edit nobody has made.

## F5 — the non-vacuity guard on the seeding vocabulary is unpinned, and fails closed

The round-8 docstring claims the assertion catches "either half emptied". It does not:
`set(vocabulary) > frozenset()` is True for any non-empty vocabulary. With the **object
half** emptied, `measure()` returns an identical census and no violations — the guard
never fires. Fail-closed rather than fail-open, so it is the lower-priority half of this
issue, but it is a sentence in a docstring asserting a property nothing checks, which is
the family.

Both found by the round-9 reader on #520; merged anyway by the owner's explicit decision,
with the findings queued here rather than taken as another review round.
