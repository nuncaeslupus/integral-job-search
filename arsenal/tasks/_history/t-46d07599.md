Found in a real candidate session (2026-09-30). Step 9 and the follow-up offer
re-read asked the candidate which technologies he knows and whether he had agent
projects. `cv/master.json` already recorded both (`skills` with levels and
provenance, `experience`, `episodes`). `rank.py` reads only `extractions/*`,
`profile/constraints.json`, `weights.json` and `traits.json`, so the stack never
reached the ranking; with thin extractions every offer sat on the frontier with
unknown dimensions and nothing said "this one wants Go, TypeScript and Kubernetes,
you are a Python engineer". The candidate asked how the tool could not know.

## Spec — `integral.stack_fit`

The contract the gate's fixtures are derived from. A fixture cites the rule
number it tests.

**R2 — whole names only.** A name counts only where it is not part of a longer
word: `Java` in `JavaScript` does not name java, `SQL` in `PostgreSQL`,
`MySQL` or `NoSQL` does not name sql, `Go` in `Google`/`Golang` does not name go
by itself (Golang does, as its own name). Letters, digits and `_` are word
characters; `+`, `#` and `.` at the edge of `C++`, `C#`, `.NET` belong to the name.

**R3 — case.** Case-insensitive, except the names marked `*`, which are also
ordinary words and count only spelled exactly as shown (`React`, not `react`;
`JS`, not `js`, so `Node.js` does not name javascript).

**R4 — `Go` is a verb too.** `Go` (exact case) does not count where it opens a
sentence (start of text, or after `.`, `!`, `?`, `:` or a line break, with only
whitespace between), where a hyphen follows it (`Go-to-market`), or where the
next word is `to`, `ahead`, `live`, `further` or `beyond`.

**R5 — what an offer names.** Its title and text, together. Named at least once
is named; no distinction between required and nice-to-have is claimed.

**R6 — what the candidate has, from `cv/master.json`.** A `skills` entry gives its
`level` to every technology its `name` names (a skill "Python (Django, FastAPI)"
gives three). A skill name is a label, not a sentence: R2 and R3 apply to it,
R4 does not (a skill named `Go` names go). Two entries naming one technology: the higher level. A level of
`null` is *unstated*. A technology named only in `experience` (title,
organisation, description) or `episodes` text is *used*, level unstated.

**R7 — what the candidate said overrides the CV.** An effective (not retracted)
`statement` row in `profile/evidence.jsonl` carrying `skill: {technology, level?,
averse?}`: the **last** such row in log order that sets `level` replaces the CV
level for that technology, downwards included, and `none` is a level. The last
row setting `averse` decides aversion. A retracted row is as if never written.
Aversion does not change the level.

**R8 — buckets, per technology the offer names.** `averse` if aversion is true;
otherwise `match` for working/strong/expert, `weak` for basic/none, `used` for a
technology known only unstated (R6), `missing` if neither CV nor statement
mentions it.

**R9 — verdict.** `unknown` when the offer names nothing; `match` when every
named technology is in `match`; `mismatch` when none is in `match` or `used`;
otherwise `partial`. Unknown is not a match: an advert that names no technology
has said nothing.

**R10 — stated, not scored.** `rank(..., stack=...)` carries each shown offer's
fit under `stack_fit`, keyed by offer id. It is not a dominance axis and moves no
offer: pricing it would be a preference, and T10's weights do not hold one.

**R11 — a step never asks for what the CV records.** Step 9 reads the fit; a
question about which technologies the candidate knows is asked only for a
technology in `missing`.

`master.json` and a stance row in, `rankings` `stack_fit` out.
