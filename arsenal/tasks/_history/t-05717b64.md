`{Filters: {inner: "{page}"}}` on its own is **refused** at load: `pagination.param` must name a *top-level* key holding the placeholder, so `_a_page_placeholder_and_a_body_field_imply_each_other` raises "carrying the page number".

Add a top-level `Page: "{page}"` beside it and the same nested value **loads and substitutes** — `_page_substituted` walks the whole structure. Measured: `{"Filters": {"inner": 2}, "ResultsPerPage": 25, "Page": 2}`.

Same shape, opposite verdict, decided by something else in the document.

## Pick one

Either nested placeholders are legal — and the lone case should load, with `pagination.param` allowed to name a path — or they are not, and the walk should substitute only the named top-level key.

Today a connector author who nests a placeholder gets a load error, adds a top-level one to satisfy it, and **silently acquires a second substitution they did not ask for**. That is the failure worth fixing: the error message teaches a workaround that changes the request.

## Chosen: nested placeholders are not legal

The walk substitutes only the key `pagination.param` names, and the load check
refuses any other position — including a nested one sitting beside a legal
top-level placeholder, which is the shape that used to load.

Both readers now come from one function, `connectors._page_placeholder_paths`.
That is the part worth keeping: the previous two readings were each defensible,
and what made the defect was that they were written separately and only agreed
on the cases somebody happened to test. `integral.page_placeholder` measures
that they still agree, over a probe table whose expected verdicts are derived
from the rule's clauses (`RULE`) rather than from a run.

Allowing `pagination.param` to name a path was the other option and was not
taken: it widens the format's one free-form structure so that a caller reading
`param` must now parse a path expression to know what varies, and it turns the
refusal into a permission at exactly the point where a contributor is already
confused. Refusing is the posture `url_pattern` and `_literal_body_violations`
already take toward a brace that does not do what it looks like.
