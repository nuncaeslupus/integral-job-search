Measured 2026-10-16/17: `tools/verified_gate.sh` (clean checkout + `make host-gate`) took 44 min on a twelve-core laptop — `9260 passed, 19 skipped in 2638.92s` — while CI's `pytest` job finishes in about two minutes. It runs on every PR that merges, docs-only included, and a review round that moves the head throws a whole run away.

`make test` already runs `pytest -n auto`, but `--dist loadfile` (pyproject addopts, deliberate: `tests/test_repo_gate.py` writes into the live tree) never splits a file across workers. So wall time is bounded by the slowest file, not by the test count. Three files alone — `tests/test_generate.py`, `tests/test_step_skills.py`, `tests/test_floor_sweep.py` — took 31 min serial for 338 tests; at least one re-runs repo-wide measurements inside its tests.

Ask:
1. Measure per-file wall time under `make test` (e.g. `--durations=0` aggregated by file, or one timed run per file) and commit the measurement, not a guess.
2. For the files on the critical path: share expensive repo-wide measurements through a session-scoped fixture or cache, or split the file so `loadfile` can spread it — without weakening what each test asserts, and without lifting `loadfile` for `test_repo_gate.py`.
3. Re-measure `make host-gate` wall time before and after; report both.

`make evidence` running 118 modules serially is T312, the other half of the same wall time; do not duplicate it here.

Suggested gate: `slowest_test_file_seconds <= 120`.
