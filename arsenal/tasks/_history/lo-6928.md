## What this is

The process-level specification, written before any individual step. Source:
`status/spec-v2-brief.md`, which carries the owner's decisions and the proposed
twelve-step list.

Deliverable is a **reviewable HTML document** plus its Markdown source, covering:

1. The step list — settle the two open questions the brief names (whether
   Intake and Constraints merge; whether Traits is its own step or folds into
   History with a coverage requirement).
2. How steps connect, including **backwards**: revising an earlier step must
   revise what later steps produced, not discard it.
3. The per-user artefact tree, and the rule that the user is identified before
   anything is read or written.
4. The offer lifecycle: statuses, purge rule, tombstones, retention.
5. The resumption model: what is on disk, and how the conversation infers where
   the candidate is without a command being typed.
6. The capture/scoring split for traits: capture is cheap and always on, scoring
   is potentially LLM-backed and runs at defined points, never per message.

## The gate

`process_spec_complete` is 1 only when the process document answers all six
items above — a document that settles the step list but leaves the offer
lifecycle or the resumption model unwritten is not complete, and S2 would then
specify steps against a process that does not exist.

S1 deliberately does **not** share a metric with S2. S2's threshold depends on
the step count this task settles, so a shared counter would make S1's gate move
when S2 was written.

## Tests

`test_process_spec_answers_every_required_item` — the six items above, each
present and non-empty; `test_the_settled_step_count_is_machine_readable` — S2's
gate divides by it, so it cannot live only in prose;
`test_every_step_in_the_list_has_a_named_gate_metric` — including
`not_implemented` for steps not yet built.

## Do not

Specify individual steps here. A step specified before the process is settled
gets the wrong boundaries, which is the whole reason for splitting S1 from S2.
