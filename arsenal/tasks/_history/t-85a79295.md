Filed from #554's second reader (round 3, non-blocking N1–N3, issuecomment-5896890208).

**N1 (fail-open against T200's own claim).** `connector_salary_audit.card_band()` slices the card with a regex that reads less than the engine's `_take`: `45.000 € - 55.000 €`, `£35,681 to £39,424` and `174K-252K USD` all return None. Outside foorilla's substituted-detail rows, a None drops the row out of the card check with nothing going red — 40 rows (usajobs 25, getmanfred 3, trabajos 3, 9 across infojobs, tecnoempleo, jobsacuk, jobfluent, greenhouse, ashby). Probe: switching jobfluent_es's engine to read 45000–45000 and copying the entry to match stays green. Closed rule: every row whose card carries money is either compared or counted, and the uncompared count is a measured key driven to 0 — not an enumeration of formats.

**N2.** foorilla rows 34 (`CAD 42K`) and 38 (`RON 100K`) are declared refused as "a single figure is not a band". T200's spec says a source salary must produce `salary.min` **or** `salary.max`, so a single figure should read. Read them, or name the engine's actual gap as the reason.

**N3 (pre-existing engine defect).** `_take` treats a following word starting with M or K as a magnitude: `USD 174,000-252,000 Mountain View` reads the top as 252,000,000,000 and `EUR 40.000-50.000 Madrid` as 5e10. In production the annual bound refuses it (fail-closed); in the audit an entry regenerated from `card_band` would certify the wrong band (fail-open), and because `card_band` reuses `_take` the card check cannot catch any `_take` bug. A magnitude suffix must be a whole token, not the first letter of a word.
