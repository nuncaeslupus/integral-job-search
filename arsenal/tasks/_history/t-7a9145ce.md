Closes the salary half of #356.

`FieldSelector` is a CSS selector plus an optional attribute, and neither can name a **part** of what it found. Two boards are blocked on exactly that:

- `landing.jobs` writes the whole band into one span — `€50.000 - €65.000`. It is the only board reachable through this format that publishes pay in euros, measured 2026-09-06 over 251 offers in a live candidate tree where 2 of 13 sources published a band at all.
- `arbeitsagentur.de` writes `1.&nbsp;Python Entwickler (m/w/d)` into every one of the three elements holding its title. DE has zero connectors.

**Not a regex.** That is the one thing this grammar exists to exclude — connector-supplied code, and a denial-of-service surface before it is anything else. Instead a closed vocabulary, `take:`, whose every member is implemented in `_take` and named by the connector: `range_low`, `range_high`, `currency`, `last_text_node`. No pattern, no template, no index crosses the file boundary.

Each member is **fail-closed**, which is #356's own negative control: text that does not have the shape the name describes yields nothing, never the unparsed string. A missing salary is a gap the candidate sees; `€250 - €350 per day, 40 hours` landing in `salary_min` is a number that is wrong and looks right.
